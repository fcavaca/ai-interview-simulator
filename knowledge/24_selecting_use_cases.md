# Selecting the right AI use case for a business (IBM AI Academy)
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C1ecR-sIE1A

## Key points
- Like a team captain calling the right play, the job is to **match the business problem to the right tool**. Winning means better outcomes and **ROI**. A wrong call means missed opportunity, lost money and brand damage.
- **Generative AI is not always the right play.** Examples:
  - A **financial forecast** usually doesn't need GenAI; classic predictive models do it for a fraction of the cost.
  - **Optimisation** problems have well-established non-GenAI methods.

  Avoid using GenAI where existing tools already work well.
- Think in both directions:
  - **tech → problem**: for a business problem, find the right technology
  - **problem → tech**: if you've already invested in AI or ML, look for use cases that existing investment can address

  Both optimise **total cost of ownership (TCO)** and ROI.
- **Four considerations**:
  1. **Needs**: do you really need GenAI, or can existing ML do it at lower TCO?
  2. **Capabilities**: do you have the right capabilities in place?
  3. **Integration**: how will it work with your existing IT stack?
  4. **Skills**: does the team have the skills to use it?
- Also look for **repeatable patterns**, consider your existing **data architecture**, and look at what's happening elsewhere in the business. Be ready to adjust as reality unfolds.

## Things an interviewer can ask
- Give an example of a business problem where GenAI is the wrong tool, and what you'd use instead.
- What four questions would you ask before approving a GenAI project?
- What do "tech to problem" and "problem to tech" mean?
