### Knowledge gaps
- **Q1 (embeddings):** An embedding turns a word or text into a **list of numbers (a vector)**, placed so that words with similar meanings sit **close together** ("apple" near "orange"). Models need it because ML algorithms **can't process raw text, only numbers**. Modern (transformer) embeddings are **contextual**: "bank" (money) and "bank" (river) get different vectors.
- **Q2 (SFT):** Pre-training gives a **base model**, an "internet document simulator" that just continues text. SFT uses the **same training algorithm** on a **new dataset of example conversations** (prompts plus ideal answers written by human labellers following "helpful, truthful, harmless" instructions). It is much cheaper (hours instead of months). The result: a model that answers like an assistant, effectively a "statistical simulation of a human labeller".
- **Q3 (RAG):** RAG **retrieves** the relevant passages from our own policy documents and puts them in the prompt (three parts: **instruction + retrieved content + question**). Benefits: answers are **grounded in our real policies** with **citations**, they stay **up to date** just by updating the documents (no retraining), and it can say **"I don't know"**. Risk: if the **retriever** finds the wrong or no passages, the answer is poor or missing.
- **Q4 (prompt injection):** Missing the **reason** LLMs are vulnerable: in normal software **code and data are separate**, but in an LLM **instructions and data are both just text**. Also missing: it is OWASP's **#1 LLM risk**, and defences like **least privilege, human in the loop, input filters**.
- **Q5 (rules + agent):** Missing the **hybrid flow**: (1) a **rules engine first** handles the clear-cut cases automatically (taxi < 500 DKK → approve; that's most claims); (2) only the cases rules can't decide go to the **AI agent**, which can read the messy photo and text; (3) the agent's recommendation passes **deterministic guardrails**, e.g. above an amount threshold a **human approves**. Rules are **deterministic** (same answer every time, auditable); the agent is **probabilistic**.
- **Q6 (diagnosis):** The **wrong total** was not explained. That's **brittle arithmetic**: models "need tokens to think" and are weak at mental maths. Fix: **offload to code execution / a calculator / Excel**.

### Incorrect answers
- **Q4:** "Direct clearly states what it wants and indirect doesn't" → Not quite. The difference is **who puts it in and where**. **Direct**: the attacker types the malicious instruction into the chat themselves. **Indirect**: the instruction is **hidden inside data the system reads**, e.g. a poisoned invoice PDF, web page or document fetched by RAG, and an **innocent user** triggers it. *(19_prompt_injection.md)*
- **Q5:** "Human would make the decision" (for every claim) → A human deciding every claim removes most of the benefit. The point of the design is that **rules decide the simple cases**, the **agent handles the messy ones**, and the **human steps in only above a threshold** or for exceptions. *(06_probabilistic_vs_deterministic.md)*
- **Q6:** "It's only a token reader" → It's a **token predictor/generator** (a very sophisticated autocomplete), not a reader. *(25_anthropic_capabilities_limitations.md)*
- **Q6:** Fix = "reinforcement learning so it understands it cannot know answers" → That's a fix **only the model builder** can do, and Karpathy describes it as adding **"I don't know" training examples** (factuality fine-tuning), not as RL. As a **user**, the course's fixes are: **verify the specifics** (check the paragraph actually exists), **ground the model in the real source** (paste the standard or use RAG/web search), **invite pushback** for sycophancy ("tell me if my assumption is wrong"), and **code execution** for the total. *(00b_karpathy_post_training_psychology.md, 25_anthropic_capabilities_limitations.md)*
